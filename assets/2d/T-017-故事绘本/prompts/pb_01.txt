Use case: one full-color page of an adult literary mystery comic book (picture book), portrait 2:3 page.
Style: adult mystery picture-book illustration. Refined ink linework with selective cross-hatching, transparent watercolor washes with opaque dark shadows, visible warm beige paper texture. Restrained realism, 1926 English country house, quiet film-noir mood. Palette: walnut brown, dark bottle green, deep wine red, paper beige, aged brass; cold blue-grey only outside windows. Warm lamp light, large areas of shadow. No saturated red anywhere. No text, no letters, no numbers, no captions, no signatures, no watermarks in the image.
CHARACTER RULES (always): The detective R. Ack is shown ONLY from behind, as a hat brim in shadow, or as his RIGHT HAND with an ivory shirt cuff and a LARGE RECTANGULAR BRASS CUFFLINK WITH A DARK-GREEN VERTICAL STRIPE — never his face, and he never appears in any mirror or photograph. Barlow (elderly silver-haired butler) always has a black silk ribbon tied over his eyes; Miss Wynn (typist, dark bob, wine-red waistcoat) always wears a green celluloid visor pulled down over her eyes; Lance (young man, brown-grey waistcoat) always has a pale silk scarf tied over his eyes. Their eyes are never visible. Anatomically correct hands. No blood, no violence.
TEXT RULE: absolutely no text, letters, numbers, signatures or handwriting anywhere. Every document, book page, telegram and newspaper shows only blank paper or faint ruling. Captions and dialogue are added later: keep the bottom 12% of the page as calm empty paper, and keep a small empty corner at bottom-right for a stack-of-telegrams counter icon.
PANEL STYLE: this page imitates an old film archive: black-and-white 4:3 archive frames with film grain and dust on a black background.

PAGE CONTENT:
Four stacked black-and-white archive frames on a black page. Frame 1: a blank black title card with a thin white border (empty, the warning text is added later). Frame 2: an old 4:3 photograph of a symmetrical English manor with a row of servants in front whose eyes are painted over with white blots. Frame 3: a blank black intertitle card with an ornamental thin white border, empty center for a five-line rhyme. Frame 4: two small old photographs side by side: a study desk with the telephone receiver lying off its hook, and an open guest register with one line struck through.
PEOPLE ON THIS PAGE: only the row of servants in frame 2, all wearing aprons or butler suits, eyes painted white. There must be NO man in a fedora or long overcoat anywhere in any photograph — the detective never appears in photographs.
